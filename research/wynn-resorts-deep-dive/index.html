How cash reaches WYNN shareholders:

  1. Macau cash goes through Wynn Macau Ltd (WML). WML pays licence and management fees to Wynn Resorts first; these are capped at $150M for 2026 and ran at $63M in H1. WML then pays dividends: about $300M in 2026, of which 72% goes to WYNN. The other 28% belongs to Hong Kong minority holders. WML carries its own $5.84B of debt.
  2. US cash goes through Wynn Resorts Finance (WRF). It is subject to the credit agreement’s restrictions on distributions.
  3. The UAE pays WYNN fees from opening, plus 40% of distributions once the $2.4B construction loan allows.

These layers matter. Consolidated Adjusted Property EBITDAR overstates WYNN’s claim, because 28% of Macau and 49.9% of the retail JV belong to others, and it ignores about $430M a year of rent, corporate cost and stock compensation.

2.3 Macau: market growth is not Wynn’s earnings growth #

The market (REPORTED, DICJ). GGR was MOP292.5B in 2019, MOP183.1B in 2023, MOP226.8B in 2024 and MOP247.4B in 2025 (85% of 2019). The first nine months of 2026 total MOP187.1B, up 3.2%, but monthly GGR has fallen YoY since June: −12.1% in June, −8.4% in July, −1.2% in August and −1.2% in September. The mix has changed. VIP baccarat was 45–49% of GGR in 2019 and 26–31% in 2025–26. Mass baccarat has been flat at about MOP34–36.5B a quarter since Q1-2024, so the 2025 growth came mostly from VIP and premium play. That is the segment where Wynn says it is “very, very prudent with credit” (Q2-26 call).

Demand quality is weakening:

  • August arrivals rose 6.1%, but overnight visitors fell 2.4% while same-day trips rose 11.7% (DSEC). GGR per visitor fell about 7% (CreditSights via AGBrief).
  • Citi’s September floor survey found premium-mass wagers down 45% YoY (AGBrief).
  • Industry commissions reached a record 20.9% of GGR in Q2 (CBRE via Macau Business).
  • Mainland Chinese outbound-investment rules took effect in July 2026. CreditSights treats them as a sentiment issue rather than a direct GGR hit.

On the positive side, October Golden Week arrivals rose 15.5% per day (AGBrief). JPMorgan nonetheless expects October GGR down 2–4% against a +15.9% comparison (AGBrief).

Wynn’s position (CALC). Wynn’s gross win divided by DICJ GGR gave a share of 13.4% in 2023, 13.1% in 2024 and 12.3% in 2025. H1-26 was 13.0% reported but only ~12.4% after normalising hold:

  • Wynn Palace mass hold was 26.6% in Q1 and 29.7% in Q2, against a trailing-eight-quarter average of ~24%. Deutsche Bank estimates the elevated hold added about $100M to Palace GGR in Q2 (AGBrief).
  • Wynn’s VIP share has fallen from about 9–10% (2023–24) to 4.7% (Q2-26) as market VIP recovered. Its mass-plus-slot share rose to 16.8% reported in Q2.
  • UBS put Wynn’s July share at 13%, down 1.5 points on June, partly from hold normalising.

From GGR to earnings (CALC, section 7). Every $1 of GGR becomes about $0.82 of net casino revenue after rebates and comps at Wynn Palace, and $0.86 at Wynn Macau. Gaming tax takes 40% of the gross (35% special tax plus 5% levies). After roughly 10% of revenue in other variable costs, each extra dollar of GGR adds about $0.33 of EBITDAR. Fixed costs are about $795M a year across the two properties and grew 6–9% in 2026 (“OpEx ex-gaming tax ~$2.9M/day, +9%”, Q2 call). With GGR flat and fixed costs up 4.5%, Macau EBITDAR falls about 3%. That is why our 2027E Macau EBITDAR ($1,096M) is below the TTM hold-normalised level ($1,152M) even though the market grows 2%.

Concession economics (REPORTED, 10-K).

  • Term: 10 years from 1-Jan-2023 to 31-Dec-2032.
  • Committed investment: MOP21.03B ($2.62B) over the term, of which MOP19.80B is non-gaming. Management describes it as $1.6B of capex and the rest opex.
  • Taxes: 35% special gaming tax plus 5% levies on GGR. The exemption from Macau’s 12% complementary tax on gaming profits runs to 31-Dec-2027; it was worth $77.1M ($0.74 a share) in 2025.
  • Minimum average GGR: MOP7M per table and MOP300k per machine.
  • Expansion: in July 2026 Macau amended the Cotai land grant to allow a new five-star hotel, theatre and event centre, for a MOP652.3M ($80.8M) premium (8-K 22-Jul-2026). The Enclave at Wynn Palace has 432 all-suite keys and costs $900–950M; construction starts 2H-26 and it opens in 2029. Management says it could add “$150 million to $175 million in EBITDA” with no gaming element (Q1-26 call).

Our model (section 8.6):

  • Base case: stabilised EBITDA before fees of $560M, between management’s low and base cases. Wynn’s take is about $134M of fees plus 40% of $426M.
  • Ramp: 65% / 85% / 100% over the first three years.
  • Financing: the JV debt costs 7%.
  • Value: the stake is worth ~$15 a share in the base case. The range runs from $8.5 (stabilised EBITDA of $400M) to $24 (management’s high case). A one-year delay costs only about $2 a share and a further $200M Wynn overrun about $2 a share.

The UAE matters, but it is not the swing factor: Macau and Las Vegas multiples move value by about $8–9 a share per turn. 2.5 Encore Boston Harbor, corporate and items hidden by consolidation #

  • Encore Boston Harbor: EBITDAR went from $257M (2023) to $237M (2025) to $222M (TTM). The margin fell from 29.7% to 25.7% in H1-26 on flat revenue and the 25% Massachusetts tax. Because the real estate was sold, $129M of cash rent (2026, +1.75% a year) comes ahead of equity. EBITDA after rent is only about $85M.
  • Corporate: “corporate expenses and other” ran $149M TTM, including about $27M at WML. Stock compensation adds $86M TTM. Both are excluded from Adjusted Property EBITDAR, and both are real.
  • Recurring exclusions: “property charges and other” ran $46–50M in 2025–TTM (2024: $215M including impairments). Pre-opening costs were $38–43M. Both are excluded from adjusted EPS every year.
  • Capitalised interest: Wynn capitalises interest on its UAE equity investment ($49.7M in FY2025, 10-K). This flatters P&L interest until opening and goes away from Q4-27.
  • Tax shields: US NOLs of $671M plus $629M of disallowed-interest carryforwards keep cash taxes near zero for several years. Foreign tax credits of $450M expire in 2027.
  • Minorities and fees: WML’s minority holds 28% of Macau. The licence fees paid to WYNN move about 28% of ~$135M a year from them to WYNN.
  • Retail JV: 49.9% is owned by Crown Acquisitions, and WYNN consolidates the JV’s $600M loan, due 2027.

Earnings-quality findings:

4. Industry structure and competitive advantage #

Barriers to entry are regulatory, and they are real.

  • Macau: six concessions (2023–2032) with table caps and minimum-GGR rules. The 2022 retender re-awarded all six incumbents.
  • Nevada: strict licensing, and the land and capital on the Strip are effectively a franchise.
  • Massachusetts: one Greater Boston licence.
  • UAE: one land-based licence per emirate, and Wynn is the only licensee to date.

Scarcity protects returns on existing assets. It does not protect against market-wide demand cycles, policy, or reinvestment competition within each market.

5. Management, governance and capital allocation #

Track record on projects (REPORTED or press):

  • Wynn Palace: due to open 25-Mar-2016, then 25-Jun-2016; it actually opened 22-Aug-2016. The main contractor’s guaranteed maximum price rose from $2.57B to $2.96B (GGRAsia, Jan-2016). By 2025 it earned $683M of EBITDAR on a total cost of about $4.1–4.2B (press estimates), a 16–17% pre-tax yield.
  • Encore Boston Harbor: the budget rose before its June-2019 opening; site clean-up alone cost $68M against a $30M estimate (Bisnow). Its EBITDAR yield on cost was roughly 9–10% before the sale-leaseback.
  • Wynn Al Marjan: the budget rose 46% from the 2023 start, and opening slipped about six months.
  • Pattern: good assets, built late and over budget.

Credibility test (GUIDANCE vs outcome). On 7-May-2026, asked about budget cushion, the CEO called higher shipping costs likely “a rounding error” on the UAE budget (Q1-26 call). Ninety days later the budget rose about $600M, half of it attributed to the conflict. His “modest delay” turned into roughly six months. In fairness, management disclosed both promptly, and the equity guidance moved by exactly the CFO’s stated $240M. Elsewhere, guidance has been reliable: capex ranges, Las Vegas opex per day (overrun by only about $0.1–0.3M a day), and the Macau capex re-phasing.

Capital allocation.

  • Positives: the dividend has been held at $1.00. WML refinanced its 2026 notes early with 6.75% 2034 notes. WRF termed out the 2027 Las Vegas notes with 6.875% 2035 notes in September 2026, before the wall.
  • Negatives: buybacks of about $1.35B at roughly $80–130 a share while committing about $1.65B of UAE equity and $0.9B to the Enclave. Leverage has not fallen: company-defined net leverage is “just over 4.4x”, Moody’s B1 rating moved to stable (Jun-2026) because Wynn has not reduced leverage “well below 6x”, and Fitch puts 2026 at 6.0x, near its downgrade threshold (AGBrief, AGBrief). Management describes buybacks as run on a “price-based grid”.

Incentives (2026 proxy).

  • Annual bonus: 70% depends on Adjusted Property EBITDAR (Macau 40%, North America 30%), 10% on Las Vegas share of Strip GGR, 10% on Forbes stars and 10% on “topping out” the UAE tower. 2025 paid out at 102.5% of target.
  • Long-term awards: at least 55% is performance-based, using relative “fair share” revenue and EBITDAR plus absolute TSR.
  • Pay: the CEO received $15.1M in 2025.
  • Gap: nothing rewards per-share FCF, ROIC, budget discipline or leverage. The metrics reward EBITDAR and construction milestones, which is exactly what has produced capex-heavy growth and flat leverage.

Ownership.

  • Large holders: Tilman Fertitta holds 12.1%. In 2025 he wrote calls on 9.0M shares at $120–200 expiring May–Sep-2026, which by our arithmetic expired unexercised. Galaxy Entertainment holds 5.0% (13G, 30-Sep-2025), a competitor stake with possible strategic meaning. Vanguard and Capital World each hold about 9–10%, and BlackRock 5.3%.
  • Selling pressure: the Elaine Wynn Family Trust sold from 9.54M shares (9.2%) to 5.82M (5.65%) by 30-Jun-2026, and remains a likely seller.
  • Insiders: directors and officers own 0.86%, with no open-market buying in the past year (Form 4 data via Yahoo).

On governance, the 2018–19 regulatory issues (Nevada and Massachusetts fines) are behind the company, and the board is independent. Leadership changed in 2026: CFO Julie Cameron-Doe retired and was replaced by Craig Fullalove, the WML CFO (8-K 9-Jan-2026).

Net assessment. Management runs the properties well, and the brand and service quality are real. Capital allocation and incentives lean toward growth and EBITDAR rather than per-share value. The “rounding error” episode is a reason to haircut UAE guidance.

6.3 Variant perception #

1. What the market believes. Macau has slowed but is basically stable. The UAE is a 2028+ option worth little today. Las Vegas is a high-quality, low-growth franchise. Sell-side estimates (FY27 EPS $4.79) are roughly right, and the stock is cheap against $129 targets.

2. What we believe instead. The market price is roughly right, but consensus earnings are about 10% too high on EBITDA and 20–30% too high on 2027 EPS. Four reasons:

  • H1-26 Macau results include about $85M of Wynn Palace mass-hold luck. Management’s “hold-adjusted” figures only correct VIP hold (Q2: $306M “VIP-normalised” against about $246M on our full normalisation).
  • Macau fixed costs are growing 6–9% against flat-to-down GGR.
  • About $5B of 2027–28 maturities and the WRF 2035 refinancing reprice coupons by 150–200bp. That adds roughly $80–100M a year of interest by 2029.
  • About $50M a year of interest capitalised on the UAE investment ends at opening, and the UAE ramp produces equity-method losses in 2027–28.

3. Why the market may be wrong. The sell side builds from management’s adjusted definitions. Those definitions exclude mass-hold variance, recurring property charges, pre-opening costs and capitalised interest, and they focus on EBITDAR rather than per-share FCF.

4. Evidence:

  • Our hold normalisation matches Deutsche Bank’s estimate of about $100M of excess GGR in Q2 at a 55% flow-through.
  • Macau opex is +9% YoY (Q2 call).
  • The debt ladder (Note 6) and the new 6.875% 2035 notes against the redeemed 5.25% notes.
  • 10-K disclosure of $49.7M of capitalised interest.
  • UBS’s July share data, which show normalisation already starting.

5. Validating or falsifying indicators:

  • Q3-26 (5-Nov): Wynn Palace mass hold reverts to 23–25%, Macau EBITDAR comes in at about $270–290M (Q3-25: $308M), and Las Vegas at about $200M. If it prints at or above $300M on normal hold, we are wrong.
  • 2027 guidance and consensus: FY27 EPS drifting from $4.79 toward about $4.0 confirms the view.
  • WML refinancing: new coupons of 7% or more confirm it.

6. Why it hasn’t been arbitraged away. Hold noise hides the trend quarter to quarter. The sell side is uniformly long, with no Holds, which creates career risk in cutting. Earnings revisions in Macau names lag the GGR data.

7. What makes the market recognise it. The 5-Nov print, 2027 commentary in February 2027, and the WML refinancing early in 2027.

Is the variant perception actionable? Not on its own. It disputes the sell side’s numbers, but the share price already sits at our base value. Expected estimate cuts argue against buying ahead of them. They don’t support a short, because the downside from here needs the bear case, not just lower EPS, and there is real positive optionality: the UAE monopoly, the Macau mass recovery, Galaxy’s 5% stake and a possible re-rating. No convincing long or short variant perception exists at $74.96, which is why the call is WATCHLIST.

11. The strongest bear case #

The argument. WYNN is a leveraged equity stub, with EV about 2.3x the market value of equity. It sits on three cyclical, policy-exposed revenue pools, at the moment when capex peaks, earnings normalise downward and financing costs reset higher.

  1. Macau is rolling over, and Wynn’s 2026 strength was luck.
    • GGR has fallen four months in a row. Premium-mass wagers were down 45% in Citi’s September survey. GGR per visitor is down about 7%, and visitation is shifting to same-day trips.
    • China’s July outbound-investment rules and tax enforcement weigh on the premium player. Industry commissions are at a record 20.9% of GGR.
    • Wynn’s VIP share has halved, and its Q2 “share gain” was mostly Wynn Palace’s 29.7% mass hold.
    • Macau fixed costs are rising 6–9%. On our cost structure, each 1% fall in GGR takes about 3% off Macau EBITDAR.
  2. Las Vegas has peaked for this cycle. Revenue has been flat since 2024 while costs per day rose about 4–6% a year. The Strip’s August growth was all baccarat hold; ex-baccarat win, visitation, occupancy and ADR fell.
  3. Encore Boston Harbor is in slow decline. The margin has fallen 4 points since 2023 behind a fixed, escalating $129M rent.
  4. The balance sheet absorbs the shock badly.
    • Lease-adjusted leverage is about 5.2x (team; Fitch about 6.0x at its downgrade threshold).
    • About $5B of 2027–28 maturities will reprice 150–200bp higher.
    • Look-through FCF is negative through 2027, with about $2.1B of capex plus $0.59B of UAE equity in 2026–27. Buybacks are being funded partly by the revolver, which had $200M drawn in H1.
  5. The UAE could become a liability rather than an option.
    • The budget is +46% since 2023 and the opening six months late, into an active regional war.
    • In H1-26, Ras Al Khaimah’s international air arrivals fell about 80% and European guests about 90%.
    • Wynn carries a joint and several completion guarantee to June 2028 alongside a sovereign Fitch has on Negative outlook.
    • Fee terms are undisclosed and may be subordinated.
  6. Consensus is too high, and the sell side is unanimously bullish. Our FY27 EPS is 22% below consensus. Further cuts and downgrades are the path of least resistance.

Quantification. The bear case (25%) loses 39% to 56%: value $32–42 against $74.96 at 12–36 months. Of the 12-month loss, about 35 points come from fundamentals (bear operations valued at today’s multiples: $48) and about 21 points from de-rating to trough multiples (Macau 7.0x, Las Vegas 9.5x). The combination that produces a 30–50% decline:

  • Macau GGR −3% in 2027;
  • hold-normalised share around 12%;
  • Macau fixed costs +3–5%;
  • Las Vegas EBITDAR −5% to −8%;
  • refinancing at about 7.75%;
  • the UAE opening in Q1-2028 at about $450M of EBITDA;
  • multiples one turn lower.

None of these is extreme on its own. The stress case (10%) adds a US recession, a 5% Macau decline and a lapse of the complementary-tax exemption. It loses about 80%, similar to WYNN’s 2008–09, 2014–15 and 2020 drawdowns.

Fair rebuttal:

  • Macau mass is stable, not falling. Mass baccarat has held at MOP34–36.5B a quarter for two years. Golden Week arrivals were +15.5%. Wynn Palace runs at 99% occupancy, and the Enclave adds 25% more rooms in 2029. The H2-26 declines partly reflect the World Cup and comparisons against a strong 2025.
  • The Las Vegas premium has held up: RevPAR +6% in H1 and group pace ahead into 2027.
  • Liquidity is ample: $4.0B at Q2, nothing due in 2026, the US 2027 notes already termed out to 2035, and WML holding $1.35B of revolver capacity plus $1.47B of cash against $1.35B of 2027 maturities.
  • The UAE’s acute phase has eased: UAE-directed fire peaked in February–April. Even management’s low case is worth about $12 a share to WYNN on our method.
  • The stock already trades at the post-reopening trough multiple (8.5x on our historical basis), and short interest is 11%. Part of the bear case is priced.

Our weighing. The rebuttals make the bear case less likely (we give it 25%, not 50%). They do not make it cheap to own at $74.96, because the price already equals the base case.

The strongest reason not to buy WYNN today: there is no margin of safety. You pay full base-case value for a leveraged, negative-FCF equity in a capex peak, just before two catalysts (the 1-Nov GGR release and the 5-Nov print) that we expect to bring hold normalisation and estimate cuts. Better information (2027 guidance, the refinancing, the UAE run-in) arrives within 6–12 months, and in our base case the stock is roughly flat over that period. Waiting costs little.

Appendix A — Methods (CALC) #

  • Hold normalisation (workbook Hold_Norm):
    • VIP win is normalised to 3.25% of turnover, the midpoint of Wynn’s 3.1–3.4% range.
    • Macau mass win is normalised to the property’s trailing average hold (up to eight prior quarters, at least four).
    • Las Vegas and EBH table win are normalised to 24% and 20%, the midpoints of their guided ranges.
    • EBITDA flow-through: Macau VIP 0.45 (after 40% tax and about 15% commissions), mass 0.55, Las Vegas 0.85, EBH 0.65. These are judgements, checked against Deutsche Bank’s estimate of about $100M of excess Q2 GGR at Wynn Palace.
  • Macau cost calibration (workbook Calib): on TTM hold-normalised data, fixed cost = revenue − 40% × GGR − 10% × revenue − EBITDAR. Fixed costs then grow with scenario inflation. Gross win includes VIP, mass and slot win, which is how Wynn’s share of DICJ GGR is defined. The MOP/USD rate is 8.06.
  • Las Vegas drivers: k = reported room revenue ÷ (rooms × days × occupancy × ADR) in 2025 (0.987). ADR is backed out of room revenue. F&B is revenue per occupied room night. Cost per day includes Nevada gaming tax.
  • UAE JV: monthly ramp (65% / 85% / 100% by year since opening, then +3% a year), averaged to annual. Interest is capitalised until opening. D&A is $165M a year. Debt is $2.4B at 7%, amortising 3% a year from the second year. Tax is 9%. Distributions are 90% of FCF from the year after opening. Wynn receives fees (24% of pre-fee EBITDA in the base case, net of a 15% service cost) plus 40% of distributions.
  • DCF: segment unlevered FCF from 2027 to 2035, mid-year discounting to 31-Dec-2026, and a Gordon terminal value in 2035. Macau cash flows after 2032 are multiplied by the probability of concession renewal. Equity = segment EVs − silo net debt (YE2026E) − minorities + UAE + other. The value is discounted 80 days to 12-Oct at the scenario cost of equity, and the Q4 dividend is added.
  • Horizon values: year-end 2027, 2028 and 2029 sum of the parts on next-year EBITDA at scenario multiples, less year-end net debt by silo, plus the UAE (stabilised value discounted if still ramping), divided by year-end diluted shares. Total return adds cumulative $1.00 dividends.
  • Verification: the Excel formulas reproduce the Python version of the model to within 1.2e-14 relative difference in all four scenarios.
