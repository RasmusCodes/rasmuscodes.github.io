Bottom line #

I applied the CoreWeave test to each public neocloud with traded debt. Recovery is derived bottom-up from each company’s filings, never assumed. The test asks whether the debt prices more default risk than the equity tail does. The cleanest way to state the answer is a breakeven recovery: the recovery at which a bond’s implied default probability equals the default probability in the deepest listed puts. If the derived recovery is above the breakeven, the debt is cheap relative to the equity.

For convertibles there are two breakevens:

  • Full model: a jump-to-default convertible tree calibrated to the listed vol surface.
  • Straight-bond bound: the convert priced as a plain bond, with the conversion option ignored. This one cannot overstate how cheap the credit is.

  1. IREN is the strongest signal, but its recovery is the least certain number in this work. Its four out-of-the-money converts trade at 54–66. The full model can only reproduce those prices with recovery of 20–30%, while my bottom-up derivation gives 57–70%. Even with the option ignored and the $5 put taken at the ask, the 1.00% 2033 (Dec) notes price more default than the puts for any recovery above 44%. The trade below needs no puts, because the bond plus the short stock already gains on default at the low recovery. It breaks even on default at about 22% recovery. The derivation leans on a management margin estimate and on owned sites valued at cost, and several known claims are left out (section 3).
  2. Nebius is a model-dependent signal. Under the full model its converts price 21–23% default to Jan-2028 against at most 12% in the puts. Ignoring the option, the 0.50% 2030s price only 9% at the derived recovery. That is no more than the puts allow. So the converts are cheap only if the market should pay for the deep out-of-the-money conversion option what listed options imply.
  3. CoreWeave’s straight notes remain the cleanest expression. That case is in the dedicated memo. CoreWeave’s own converts follow the Nebius pattern: breakevens of 12–18% under the full model but 57–69% on the straight-bond bound.

2. Method, and what changed from the CoreWeave analysis #

Recovery. I used the same structure as the CoreWeave model. Value at default is made up of four parts:

  • PV of cash EBITDA still to come from the contracted backlog. Prepaid revenue brings no new cash.
  • Residual GPU value at the end of the backlog.
  • Owned real estate.
  • Receivables and restricted cash.

Secured debt and 5% administrative costs come off the top. What remains is shared by notes, six months’ interest, trade claims and capped lease-rejection claims.

There are three scenarios: liquidity default, loss of the largest customer, and AI-compute bust. They are weighted 25/50/25 (harsh 10/45/45, mild 40/45/15) and averaged over default dates to the put expiry. The generic code reproduces CoreWeave at 32%, against 34% in the dedicated model. Additions for Nebius and IREN:

  • Cash on hand and money raised after the balance-sheet date are assumed spent on capex before any default. They become GPUs and owned sites, valued at the same haircuts. Without this, IREN’s and Nebius’s large cash piles would count as neither assets nor claims.
  • Delivery of the backlog is capped by the GPUs actually funded in each scenario, as in the dedicated CoreWeave model.
  • Accreting claims for Nebius, whose notes accrete to 110–125% of principal.
  • Non-core stakes, valued with scenario haircuts.
  • A sensitivity for refund claims by a lost customer that had prepaid.

Default probability from the converts. The convertible tree uses jump-to-default, with hazard rising as the stock falls (beta 1.5). The diffusive vol is recalibrated so the tree still reprices the listed Jan-2029 ATM call. “Below model range” means the bond trades below the lowest price the model can produce at that recovery. The model then cannot explain the price, so the bond is cheap at that recovery.

One feature matters for long-dated, far out-of-the-money converts. In this model a higher hazard also raises the drift of the surviving stock, so the conversion option keeps 15–20 points of value even at high hazard. For example, the Nebius 2034s are worth at least about 81 at 30% recovery. The market evidently pays less for that option, which is why the straight-bond bound is reported alongside.

Default probability from the puts. I used the Carr-Wu single-strike estimate. For Nebius and IREN the deepest liquid strikes are 25% and 20% of spot. Those puts also pay on deep declines without default, so their figure is only an upper bound on the equity-implied default probability. The breakeven test uses the highest put-based estimate available, so it is conservative toward “cheap”.

Trade construction:

  • Long $1mm face of the most credit-sensitive liquid convert.
  • Short the full model delta.
  • Puts at about 20% and 37% of spot, sized so the position neither gains nor loses on default at the low recovery. No puts if it already gains there.

Risks and what would change the view:

  • Recovery: the derivation is the weakest link; see the sensitivities above.
  • Hedge ratio: if the notes begin trading on a higher recovery, their sensitivity to the stock falls. The short then over-hedges. If the bond moves half as much as the model, a 50% stock rally costs about $80k [C].
  • Execution: busted converts trade in size infrequently, and the 0% 2031s print under two CUSIPs.
  • Funding the short: short interest is about 20% of shares [C], and IREN has a $6bn at-the-market equity program [F].
  • Dated checks:
    • The September-quarter 10-Q, due around November 2026, will show Mackenzie and subsidiary draws and any conversions of the 2029/2030 notes.
    • Microsoft acceptance of Horizons 2–4 is targeted for Q4 2026, with contractual grace to the start of Q2 2027 [F].

4. Nebius #

Capital structure.

  • Converts: eight series, $13,425M of original principal plus a $5.3M legacy remnant [F: 6-K 2026-08-12 Note 12; 6-K 2026-08-24]. All are parent senior unsecured, and principal accretes to 110–125% at maturity.
    • In the money: the June-2025 2029 and 2031 series ($87.5M each, conversion price $51.45) and the Sept-2025 1.00% 2030 and 2.75% 2032 ($1,581.25M each, $138.75).
    • Out of the money: the March-2026 1.25% 2031 ($2,587.5M, $183.22) and 2.625% 2033 ($1,750M, $180.31), and the August-2026 0.50% 2030 ($3,450M, $313.46) and 4.50% 2034 ($2,300M, $324.65).

    • $3.5bn Citi 364-day bridge signed 2026-10-17, secured on substantially all assets of the parent and the shares of its two main subsidiaries. Draws are undisclosed [F: 6-K 2026-10-22].
  • Cash: $8,042.1M plus $1,056.0M restricted at 2026-06-30 [F]. The August converts added about $5.68bn net [F].

Nebius’s contracted cash flow after default is small, for two reasons:

  • Prepayments: customers prepay heavily. I net about $10.4bn: deferred revenue plus about $4.45bn of H2-2026 prepayments implied by the guidance [C].
  • Margin: the 50% segment margin leaves little after that.

Its value sits instead in the roughly $21bn of cash and new money that becomes GPUs and owned sites before any default [C].

Owned-site share and haircuts:

  • 19% of new capex goes to owned sites. The derivation is 25% data-center share × 75% owned [J]. It is consistent with about $11.3M per MW of shell cost [S: JLL via a secondary source] against about $29M per MW of GPUs [F: IREN’s Dell order, $5.8bn for 200 MW].
  • The ClickHouse haircut is 29% [S: EquityZen end-2025 average secondary discount], plus a 58.6% software-index drawdown in the bust [S: WisdomTree, BVP Nasdaq Emerging Cloud Index].

Risks and what would change the view:

  • Option valuation: the signal depends on how the conversion option is valued; the straight bound says the 0.50% 2030s are roughly fair.

5. CoreWeave #

The dedicated memo is CoreWeave: own the unsecured notes, buy default insurance in the equity tail. Its key figures:

  • Derived recovery 34% (24–40%).
  • 9.75% 2031 notes at 81.23 price 31% default to Jan-2029, against 19–26% in the puts [M, C].
  • Markit 5-year CDS closed at 1,140.6bp on 2026-10-22 [M: LSEG].
  • The recommended structure is long notes, puts and a short stock hedge, carrying about 7% a year.

The same converts test on CoreWeave’s own converts gives a generic recovery of 32% (23–39%):

  • Full-model breakevens: 12–18% against the 26% high end of the puts.
  • Straight-bond breakevens: 57–69%.

So the converts, like Nebius’s, are cheap only under the full model. The straight notes are the better instrument. A converts version of the trade, at $1mm face, would be long the 1.75% 2032s at 67.87 and short 6,521 shares, with 68 × $10 and 35 × $20 Jan-2029 puts. It carries about 0.9% a year and makes +$90k on default at 32% recovery.

6. What the three cases say together #

  • The cheapness shows up where the debt is furthest from the money. The busted converts at IREN and Nebius’s August 2026 notes are where the equity no longer supports the bond. CoreWeave’s straight notes are the other case.
  • Recovery drives the answer, and it differs a lot by business model:
    • CoreWeave leases its data centers and would have about $23bn of secured debt ahead of the notes in a 2027 liquidity default, giving 34%.
    • Nebius has sold much of its future cash flow through prepayments and is adding secured debt, giving 40%.
    • IREN owns its sites and power, giving 64%.
  • The market does not price that difference. Each company’s deepest out-of-the-money convert can be fitted by the full model only at recoveries of 20% or less: CoreWeave’s 2033s, Nebius’s 2034s and IREN’s 2033 (Dec). The gap to the derived recovery is largest at IREN.
