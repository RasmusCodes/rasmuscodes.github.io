Forensic screen: AAOI, Coherent and Lumentum

AAOI is the clear outlier: one distributor, Digicomm, was 53% of 2025 revenue and 67–72% of receivables. Coherent’s earnings quality fell sharply; Lumentum is the cleanest.

As of 2026-10-28. Latest filings used: AAOI’s 10-Q for Q2 2026 (filed 2026-08-06), COHR’s FY2026 10-K (filed 2026-08-14) and LITE’s FY2026 10-K (filed 2026-08-17). All three report their next quarter in early November.

Bottom line #

  • AAOI is the clear outlier. Its M-score has sat above the −1.78 Beneish cutoff for seven straight quarter-ends. Its Montier C-score hit the maximum 6 in FY2024 and FY2025. Operating cash flow stayed negative while trailing revenue growth ran at 50–100%. The core issue is one distributor, Digicomm. Digicomm was 53% of 2025 revenue and 67–72% of receivables, on what management calls “longer than typical payment terms.” The SEC asked about exactly this in April 2026. A prior material weakness and a May 2026 auditor change from Grant Thornton to PwC complete the picture. None of this proves misstatement, but AAOI’s reported revenue is only as good as Digicomm’s payments.
  • COHR has no classic manipulation signal, but FY2026 earnings quality fell sharply. Net earnings were $787m against $80m of operating cash flow. One-off and non-cash items, namely a business-sale gain, an investment gain and a deferred-tax benefit, add up to about half of earnings. Inventory rose 80% against 22.5% revenue growth. The C-score is 6/6 and the M-score has drifted from −2.64 to −2.00, now in the grey zone. The SEC made COHR remove several non-GAAP add-backs in December 2024.
  • LITE is the cleanest once a $7.76bn non-cash loss is excluded. The loss came from swapping convertible notes for shares. Without it, operating cash flow ($751m) is close to net income ($822m, flattered by a $236m tax-allowance release). Receivable days are flat and inventory days are falling. Its M-score sits above the cutoff only because revenue grew 83%. Accruals contribute almost nothing.

How to read the scores:

  • Beneish M (8-variable). Above −1.78 is the original “likely manipulator” cutoff, and −2.22 to −1.78 is a commonly used grey zone. The 5-variable version uses −2.22.
  • Dechow F-score. 1.0 means the average misstatement probability in the authors’ sample. The usual bands are above 1.85 for substantial risk and above 2.45 for high risk.
  • Montier C-score. Six yes/no warning signs; 5–6 is high.
  • Piotroski F. 8–9 is strong and 0–2 is weak.
  • Altman Z. Below 1.81 is distress and above 2.99 is safe. For Z’’ the bands are 1.10 and 2.60.
  • Sloan accruals. Net income minus operating cash flow over average assets. Persistently above roughly +10% predicts weaker future earnings.

“Ex-ext” means excluding gains and losses on debt extinguishment. LITE FY2026 and AAOI FY2024 booked large non-cash losses swapping convertible notes for stock, which would otherwise make accruals look artificially clean.

1. AAOI (Applied Optoelectronics) #

The scores. The ex-ext trailing-twelve-month M-score crossed −1.78 at December 2024 and peaked at +0.23 at June 2025. It has eased to −1.19 since. At the peak, the drivers were the receivables index (DSRI 2.06), growth (SGI 1.78) and accruals (TATA 0.14). The latest reading leans more on growth and on asset quality (AQI 2.08). AQI rose because $324m of equipment prepayments now sit in “other assets.”

The other screens agree:

  • Montier C-score was 6/6 in FY2024 and FY2025.
  • Sloan accruals were +15.9% of average assets in FY2025, against negative readings in every earlier year.
  • Dechow F-score was 1.82 for FY2025, just under the 1.85 “substantial” band, and peaked near 2.4 on a trailing basis in mid-2025.
  • Altman Z’’ sat in the distress zone from FY2021 to FY2024. It recovered in FY2025 largely because equity raises lifted cash and book equity.

The Digicomm receivable. Digicomm is a cable-TV equipment distributor.

Digicomm 2023 2024 2025 H1 2026
Share of revenue 11.3% 34.1% 53.1% 42.8%
Share of receivables at period end n/a 66.6% 71.6% 67.2% ($211.0m)

At June 2026 Digicomm owed $211.0m against $147.0m of first-half sales. That is roughly 240–260 days of its sales, compared with roughly 85–95 days for AAOI’s other customers. Management’s explanation, added after the SEC asked, is that it extended “longer than typical payment terms” so Digicomm holds inventory ready for cable operators’ network builds. Revenue recognized on shipments to a distributor that is carrying stock on extended credit is the classic setup for pulled-forward revenue. Collection history has been good so far, per the company. The thing to watch is whether Digicomm receivables convert to cash, or keep growing faster than Digicomm sales.

There is also a small internal inconsistency in the FY2025 10-K. It gives Digicomm’s 2024 revenue share as 34.1% in the business section and 35.1% in the notes.

Cash, capex and dilution.

  • Cash burn. Operating cash flow was −$174m in 2025 and −$132m over the last twelve months.
  • Capacity spend. Capex was $460m plus $300m of equipment prepayments over the last twelve months, about 22 times depreciation.
  • Funding. About $1.35bn of at-the-market equity was raised over the last twelve months, and the share count is up 36% in a year.
  • Customer warrant. Amazon holds a warrant for up to 7.9m shares at $23.70. It vests with up to $4bn of purchases and is booked as a reduction of revenue.

Moving to a Big Four auditor is generally a positive. The concern is the cluster: a weakness, an SEC review focused on receivables, and an auditor change, all within twelve months of a concentration build-up.

Text. AAOI’s 10-K Fog index is about 20, flat for years, so readability gives no signal. Its 10-K is the stickiest of the three, with 86% of 5-word phrases carried over from the prior year. The new risk factors in the FY2025 10-K cover the AI-driven US manufacturing build-out (overcapacity, impairment, stranded assets) and tariffs. None was added on Digicomm or receivable concentration.

2. COHR (Coherent) #

The scores. The M-score rose steadily from −2.64 to −2.00 since early 2025. The drivers are accruals turning positive (TATA +0.04), growth, receivables (DSRI 1.14) and a falling depreciation rate. The depreciation rate fell because property, plant and equipment jumped 60% with assets not yet in service. The other screens:

  • Montier C-score was 6/6 for FY2026.
  • Dechow F-score was 1.71, above average.
  • Sloan accruals turned positive (+4.3%) for the first time in the series.
  • Piotroski F is 7 of 9, because profitability, margins and leverage all improved.

Where the earnings came from. FY2026 consolidated net earnings were $786.9m and operating cash flow was $79.5m.

  • Non-operating items. Earnings include a $124.1m gain on the sale of the aerospace and defense business, a $74.0m gain on an equity investment, and a $198.7m deferred-tax benefit. Together these add up to about half of earnings.
  • Working capital. Inventory absorbed $1,183m. Inventory days went from 133 to 187 and inventory rose 80% against 22.5% revenue growth. Receivables absorbed $368m and other operating assets $408m.
  • Payables. Payables provided $749m, with payable days going from 79 to 138. Without that stretch, operating cash flow would have been negative. Payables also include about $372m of unpaid capex.
  • Funding. Free cash flow was −$1.02bn, funded by about $2.0bn of new common stock in the January–March 2026 quarter and a new $1.25bn term loan.
  • Disclosure. Management’s MD&A explains the inventory build in one sentence, “to support higher revenue growth.”

SEC and audit. COHR went through a five-letter SEC review from April to December 2024 over non-GAAP add-backs. In December 2024 it agreed to stop excluding inventory write-offs, overlapping labor and travel, and the manufacturing inefficiencies of closing sites. EY has been auditor since 2008 and its opinion is clean. The only critical audit matter is goodwill impairment for the Lasers unit. The FY2026 10-K added a generic internal-control risk factor, but no control deficiency is disclosed.

Text. Negative words in COHR’s MD&A rose every year, from 0.66% in FY2021 to 1.65% in FY2026, even as earnings rose. Its risk-factor Fog is the highest of the three and rising (22.4). The MD&A was heavily rewritten: only 51% of 5-word phrases carried over from FY2025. New risk paragraphs focus on demand cancellations, excess or obsolete inventory if forecast orders do not materialize, contract-manufacturer qualification, indium phosphide supply and tariffs. That is consistent with the inventory build above.

3. LITE (Lumentum) #

The $7.76bn loss. In April and May 2026, LITE swapped $1.12bn of 2026, 2028 and 2029 convertible notes for 10.6m shares in private exchanges. The conversion value above principal was booked as a $7,756.6m non-cash, non-deductible loss. Reported net income was −$6.94bn and the reported M-score −6.58. Both are artifacts. Excluding the loss, net income is $822m, of which $236m is a valuation-allowance release.

The scores, ex-ext. The M-score is −1.62, above the cutoff, but the decomposition shows why. Sales growth adds +0.74, while accruals add only +0.04, receivable days are flat at about 47–54 days, and gross margin expanded (GMI 0.67). This is the model’s well-known false positive for hypergrowth. The other screens:

  • Dechow F-score is 0.83, below average risk.
  • C-score is 4.
  • Piotroski F is 6.
  • Sloan accruals are +1.2%.
  • Cash flow. Operating cash flow is $751m and free cash flow is +$300m.
  • Working capital. Inventory days fell from 142 to 119. Payable days rose from 57 to 98, partly from $181m of unpaid capex.

Other items.

  • NVIDIA preferred. NVIDIA bought $2.0bn of Series A convertible preferred on 2026-03-02 at $695.31 per share. LITE’s two largest end customers are unnamed, at 26.6% and 15.0% of FY2026 revenue. One customer is 30.4% of receivables, up from 13.2%.
  • Export-control inquiry. In August 2024 LITE received BIS and DOJ subpoenas concerning past Huawei shipments.
  • Accounting leadership. The chief accounting officer stepped down in February 2026 ahead of retirement. onsemi’s CFO, Thad Trent, joined the board and audit committee in December 2025.
  • SEC reviews. The 2024 review covered non-GAAP labelling and MD&A quantification, and the 2025 review covered segment disclosures. Both were routine.
  • Auditor. Deloitte, auditor since 2016. The only critical audit matter is inventory valuation.

Text. Fog is stable at about 20.4 for the whole 10-K. The MD&A reads at 20.8, its highest in the series. Uncertainty words in the MD&A rose from 1.16% to 1.72% of words over five years.

4. Readability, tone and text change across the three #

  • Fog index. Every 10-K since FY2021 scores 20.0–20.9 on the whole document, the normal range for 10-Ks, and no company’s readability moved enough to matter. Li (2008) links harder-to-read reports to lower and less persistent earnings, but the spreads here are a fraction of a grade level.
  • Tone (Loughran-McDonald word lists). COHR’s MD&A has turned steadily more negative. LITE’s has become more uncertain. AAOI’s has barely changed.
  • Year-over-year change (“Lazy Prices”). COHR rewrites the most, with 51% MD&A carry-over in FY2026, and AAOI the least, with 87%. In the Lazy Prices research, large changes predict weaker returns. For AAOI, the absence of change while the business transformed is itself a gap in disclosure.
  • Benford’s law. Tagged amounts in each 10-K mostly fit first-digit expectations. LITE FY2024 is the exception, with too many leading 1s (p = 0.0004), and LITE FY2025 is marginal (p = 0.017). That is consistent with many related balances near $1bn, and the test is weak at about 500 correlated numbers, so treat it as noise.

5. What to check in the November prints #

  • AAOI. Digicomm’s share of receivables and revenue, and whether Digicomm receivable days shorten. Operating cash flow ex-prepayments. Inventory days, now 183. Any change in revenue-recognition or allowance language under the new auditor. The first PwC-reviewed quarter is Q3 2026.
  • COHR. Whether inventory days come back down from 187 as the 1.6T ramp ships. Payable days at 138 cannot keep rising. Operating cash flow against net income without one-off gains. Inventory reserves and write-offs, now that write-offs can no longer be adjusted out of non-GAAP.
  • LITE. Further convert exchanges, which would mean more non-cash losses. Concentration of its largest customer, at 30.4% of receivables. Any outcome of the BIS/DOJ matter.

Method and caveats #

  • Data. All figures come from SEC XBRL company facts and the filed documents. Trailing-twelve-month figures are fiscal year plus year-to-date minus the prior year-to-date. Quarter-basis working-capital days use the latest quarter’s sales or COGS × 91.25.
  • Hand fixes. LITE tags “Accounts receivable, net” as a contract-asset concept, and its cash-flow change matches. LITE’s $1,999.7m preferred is carried in paid-in capital; XBRL shows only its par value. AAOI switched its capex tag in 2026.
  • Hand checks. The FY2025 AAOI, FY2026 COHR and FY2026 LITE M-scores were recomputed by hand and match. Balance sheets and cash-flow statements were checked against the filed 10-K and 10-Q text.
  • Model limits. These models were estimated on older, mostly steady-state samples. Hypergrowth (sales-growth index), capex booms (asset-quality and depreciation indices, Dechow’s broad accruals) and acquisitions all push scores toward “risk” without any misstatement. All three companies are in an AI-driven growth and capex cycle, so read the components rather than the headline score.
  • Readability. Computed in-house. Numeric tables are removed using the Loughran-McDonald 15%-digit rule. Syllables come from the Loughran-McDonald dictionary, then the CMU dictionary, then a heuristic; on a labelled test the counter matched 82 of 84 words and classified every complex word correctly. Absolute Fog levels can differ by about one point from other tools, but comparisons across these filings are like-for-like.
  • Gaps. A few 2020–2021 10-Qs lack tagged PP&E, so some early quarterly M-scores are missing. Insider trading, short interest, earnings-call transcripts and a full non-GAAP reconciliation were not analysed.
