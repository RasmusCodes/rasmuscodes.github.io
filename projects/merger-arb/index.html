A merger-arbitrage lab

Is the 21.5% spread on Marvell’s all-stock bid for Lumentum a mispricing or fair pay for the risk? Verified deal terms, a break-price model, scenarios through a margin account, and a manually approved execution tool.

On 2 November 2026 Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum for a fixed 4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share, with closing guided for the first half of 2028. Three weeks later Lumentum trades 21.5% below the value of those shares. Merger arbitrage buys Lumentum and shorts 4.5 Marvell shares per share, collecting the gap if the deal closes. A spread that wide on a signed deal between two large companies looks like an opportunity, so the first job was to check it is not a data error and then to work out what it pays for.

It is not a data error. The 4.50 ratio comes from the merger agreement itself. The closing prices match an independent Cboe download exactly and agree with Google Finance. The stale after-hours quotes some sources show are flagged and ignored.

It is wide for reasons. Closing takes about 16 months. China’s merger regulator is the main approval risk, and it has let US chip deals lapse before. Both sets of shareholders are unhappy, and Lumentum’s vote needs a majority of all outstanding shares. The whole AI-semiconductor complex has just fallen 25-50%. Borrowing Marvell is cheap and plentiful, so the short leg does not explain the spread.

Antitrust is the crux, and China is the weak link. The combined company would be the dominant merchant supplier of the DSP chips inside optical modules (a Chinese module maker’s prospectus puts Broadcom and Marvell at about 90% of that market). It would also be a laser and module rival of the Chinese firms that buy those chips. That is the profile China’s regulator approved only with conditions after 13-18 months in Nvidia-Mellanox and AMD-Xilinx. It let four US deals time out when tensions were high. Gate by gate (US 93%, both shareholder votes 81%, EU 94%, China 69%), completion comes to about 48%, essentially the market’s own estimate.

How antitrust worries move the stocks. Marvell fell about 21% on the deal, so bad regulatory news lifts Marvell more than it sinks Lumentum. If three quarters of that discount would reverse on a break, a US lawsuit moves Marvell about +12% and Lumentum slightly up too. The hedged position loses about $21 per Lumentum share for every 10-point drop in the market’s completion odds, whichever leg moves. Past deals behaved the same way: surprise blocks blew spreads out by 12-130 points while the acquirers rallied, and approvals tightened them by about 8 points.

Verdict: potentially attractive, small size only. If the deal closes, the trade earns about $101 per Lumentum share. If it breaks, the result depends on where Lumentum would trade on its own and how much of Marvell’s drop reverses. A triple check changed the call from “no trade” for two reasons. The model had left out the $3.2bn fee Marvell owes Lumentum if regulators kill the deal. And its base case for Lumentum’s break value used the factor model’s estimate as the best case: $613, which matches how 49 chip stocks moved against the same model in this crash, ±15%. Corrected, the trade earns about 5% a year above Treasuries at 48% odds, just over a 5% hurdle. The catch is that the market prices Lumentum’s break value at only $494-534; if the market is right, the trade is roughly fair, and under the stress case it loses. So the dashboard reads WATCH, and a $10k account should hold at most one 2-for-9 lot.

The dashboard below recomputes everything in your browser from the sliders: the spread, completion and break payoffs, break-even probability, what each regulatory headline does, scenarios simulated day by day through an IBKR-style margin account, survival-first position sizing and a Monte Carlo. It has no trading functionality. The repository also contains a local-only execution tool for Interactive Brokers: paper by default, typed approval, short leg first, partial-fill and unhedged-leg alerts, an alert-only spread-trigger ladder and a no-orders readiness check. The triple check found and fixed serious safety bugs in that tool (for example, “cancel all” could re-send an order); it still needs a paper-account test against real IBKR software.

How it was built #

  • Deal terms come from the merger agreement: the ratio, the absence of a collar, the outside-date chain to 1 August 2028, a $3.2bn regulatory reverse fee, the vote standards, the efforts covenant and dividend policy. A new deal is a new data file.
  • Break prices project both stocks forward from their unaffected 28 October prices with factor models estimated before any deal news. Resetting to the unaffected prices would be wrong after a sector crash: Lumentum would “nearly double” on a break, and so would Marvell.
  • Comparables: 21 large US deals from CRSP, with terms validated by checking that each completed deal’s spread converged to zero. Only Anthem/Cigna, later blocked in court, traded wider at the same stage.
  • Risk: IBKR’s published margin, interest and borrow rules. At full Reg T leverage, a Marvell rally while Lumentum lags triggers a margin call even in a scenario where the deal later closes.
