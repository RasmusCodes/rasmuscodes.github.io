A credit and capital-structure lab

Reading a company’s default risk off every security it has issued, with an interactive bankruptcy waterfall built from the filings.

A company’s loans, bonds, convertibles, preferred stock, equity, CDS and options are all claims on the same assets. If the markets for them price the company’s default risk differently, one of them is wrong, or the difference pays for something else. This lab models the capital structure from the filings, prices every security on one asset process, and compares the result with what each market implies.

Pick a company below, move the value it is worth in a default, and watch who gets paid. The sliders change leverage, maturities, the asset value and volatility, bankruptcy costs and departures from absolute priority; the simulation runs 2,000 default scenarios through the waterfall.

What the case studies found #

Market data from the close of 20 November 2026; balance sheets from the latest 10-Q or interim statements.

  • CoreWeave. The senior notes recover 35% in the central default scenario (21-50% across the range) after the SPV term loans take their GPU collateral and the notes share the operating subsidiaries with trade and lease claims. At that recovery the notes imply a 60% chance of default within five years; a structural model calibrated to the equity says 65% and prices the notes 5-8 points below where they trade. The Jan-2029 $5 puts cap the risk-neutral default probability by then at 16%, against 32-34% implied by the notes.
  • Strategy. Its assets are mostly bitcoin, so the asset value the models usually have to guess can be measured. Priced off the observed assets and their volatility, the model values the equity within 1.5% of the market. Calibrated the usual way, from the equity, it gets assets 22-35% too low and asset volatility twice too high.
  • Wolfspeed’s 2025 bankruptcy. The engine reproduces the court-filed liquidation analysis exactly. The confirmed plan then gave unsecured creditors 25-30%, where strict priority at the plan’s own valuation gave about 1%: make-wholes, a rights offering, trade claims paid in full and a 5% gift to old shareholders are what negotiation adds.
  • Nebius and IREN. Their convertibles imply at most 26-51% five-year default probabilities; equity-calibrated structural models say 73-75%.

None of these gaps is an arbitrage. Each signal in the lab’s scanner lists what can keep it open: stale or thin bond prices, recovery uncertainty, the cost of shorting bonds, illiquid options, and answers that depend on the model.

How it works #

  • The waterfall splits value across legal entities, lets collateral pay its secured lenders, gives recourse lenders deficiency claims, handles guarantees, subordination with turnover, lease-rejection caps and bankruptcy costs, and can carve out a gift to equity.
  • Structural models: Merton, Black-Cox and CreditGrades, plus a pricer that values every claim of a real waterfall on one asset process, exact to quadrature.
  • Reduced-form models: hazard curves from bonds, an ISDA-style CDS converter that matches Markit’s CoreWeave quote to 0.02 points, convertibles with jump to default, and model-free default bounds from deep puts.
  • History: rating-transition matrices and default rates from S&P’s regulatory filing, compared with what the markets imply.

Limits #

Structural models need an asset value nobody observes, and assume lognormal assets and a static debt stack; real bankruptcies are negotiated. Bond prices are last trades, the CDS curve is one documented point, and recoveries move most with a judgment input, the value at default. The documentation sets out where each of these bites.
