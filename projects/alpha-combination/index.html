Combining sixteen equity signals: does complexity pay?

A pre-registered test of seventeen ways to combine noisy, correlated return signals in the S&P 500 from 1980 to 2024, a second test that adds analyst and options signals, and an interactive lab that runs the same methods in the browser.

A portfolio manager with many imperfect signals has to decide how to combine them. Machine learning promises to find the right weights, interactions and regimes; equal weighting promises nothing and is hard to beat. I tested seventeen combination methods against equal weights on sixteen published US-equity signals (momentum, reversal, value, quality, investment, low risk and seasonality) in the S&P 500, strictly out of sample and after trading costs. The protocol, hypotheses and decision rule were committed to git before any result existed.

The lab below has three parts. The first shows why ten highly correlated signals are not ten sources of alpha. The second builds a world where you know the truth (pick the signals, their strength and correlations, add non-linearity, regime switches or decay) and runs a full walk-forward comparison of the methods in your browser. The third shows the S&P 500 results.

What I found #

Question Result
Does any complex method beat equal weights? Not by the pre-registered rule: no method clears a Holm-adjusted 5% test on net Sharpe or on forecast accuracy
Do complex models forecast better? Slightly. Random forests raise the mean monthly rank IC from 0.028 to 0.042; tests that allow for the dependence between methods accept the gain (SPA p = 0.013), the stricter Holm test does not (p = 0.09)
Do better forecasts make better portfolios? Not after costs. Flexible models trade about twice as much (10.6-13.5 times the book a year against 6.2), and their net Sharpe gains of 0.1-0.2 are not significant
What did best after costs? A mean-variance mix of the signal portfolios: net Sharpe 0.75 against 0.27. A minimum-variance mix that ignores expected returns does as well, so the gain comes from the covariance of the signals, not from forecasting them
And once market exposure is removed? Equal weights carries a market beta of about -0.5 from its low-risk signals. Hedged ex ante, its net Sharpe is 0.78 and no method beats it
Did the edge last? No. Equal weights’ rank IC fell from 0.037 in 1980-99 to 0.004 in 2015-24, and every method faded with it
Do analyst and options signals change this? No. In a second pre-registered test the analyst signals raise equal weights’ rank IC by about a third, but no complex method beats equal weights on 20 or 23 signals, and after 2004 five estimated linear methods do significantly worse once the market is hedged

Complexity cut both ways. Less shrinkage and deeper trees always helped: sixteen weights are well estimated from 350,000 stock-months. More adaptivity always hurt: forgetting factors, regime-specific weights and dynamic model averaging tracked noise. Regressions give the 52-week-high signal a negative weight: it is 58% correlated with momentum and adds nothing alone, so it is used to hedge momentum, against its published sign.

The machinery passes its own test. On synthetic data with a strong signal, gradient boosting recovers 86% of a non-linear truth where linear models get 58%, and the regime ensemble recovers 98% of a regime-switching one. At realistic signal strength most of these advantages shrink into the noise across seeds, which is what the real data look like.

New information helped equal weights, not complexity. The obvious objection is that sixteen signals that behave like ten are too similar for a flexible model to find anything. So a second pre-registered test added four I/B/E/S analyst signals (forecast revisions, revision breadth, earnings surprises, forecast dispersion) and three OptionMetrics signals (volatility skew, call-put volatility spread, realized minus implied volatility). With all seven the effective number of independent signals rises from 10 to 14. The analyst signals raise equal weights’ rank IC from 0.018 to 0.024 in 1990-2024 (Holm p = 0.06). The largest complex-method gain, mean-variance’s +0.61 net Sharpe, fails the Holm test (p = 0.21), and much of it is market exposure: hedged, it trails equal weights (0.54 against 0.66). From 2004, with the options signals, every complex method trails equal weights.

How it works #

  • Point-in-time data. S&P 500 members as of each month from CRSP (no survivorship bias, delisting returns included) and Compustat fundamentals usable six months after the fiscal year end. 539 out-of-sample months, 1980-2024. The second test adds unadjusted I/B/E/S forecasts (scaled by same-date prices, blanked across stock splits) and the OptionMetrics volatility surface.
  • One signal interface. Every signal arrives as records with instrument, date, horizon, value, confidence, observations, provenance and a code-hash version, so a new signal (or a vendor file) plugs in without code changes.
  • Seventeen methods behind one interface: equal and family weights, IC weights, OLS, ridge, lasso, elastic net, principal components, signal mean-variance, Bayesian model averaging over all 65,536 subsets, Bayesian shrinkage toward equal weights, random forests, gradient boosting, dynamic model averaging, a regime-conditioned ensemble and two online learners.
  • No peeking. Walk-forward refits every year, hyperparameters chosen by nested validation inside each training window, a one-month embargo, and a test that changes everything after a date and checks no earlier forecast moves.
  • Statistics for many tests. Ledoit-Wolf Sharpe tests, Holm, Benjamini-Hochberg and Romano-Wolf corrections, Hansen’s SPA test, deflated Sharpe ratios that count every configuration in an append-only experiment registry, and the probability of backtest overfitting.

Limits #

  • Large caps, monthly horizon, sixteen classic signals (twenty-three in the second test). Machine learning gains are documented to be larger among small stocks and with hundreds of characteristics; this says nothing about that setting.
  • Trading costs follow an assumed schedule by era, not measured trades, and these monthly full-universe books have small capacity (tens of millions of dollars under a square-root impact model).
  • The market-hedging and mean-variance decompositions were decided after seeing the main results and are labelled exploratory. The second test made the market hedge confirmatory.
  • Analyst and options data cover shorter periods, so the second test has 419 months with analyst signals and only 251 with options signals.

How it’s built #

A Python package (NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn, LightGBM) with 41 tests, checkpointed experiment runners and a registry of every run. The lab is a JavaScript port of the methods that runs in a Web Worker; 29 checks confirm it reproduces the Python library’s weights and forecasts to about one part in a trillion. The CRSP, Compustat, I/B/E/S and OptionMetrics data are licensed and not published; the lab shows only portfolio-level results.
