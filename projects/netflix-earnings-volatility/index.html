Netflix earnings-volatility study

Does NFLX really trade flat between earnings? Eleven years of releases, peers and option prices say no.

RS Rasmus Suonio
25 Oct 2026 · 1 min read
Done Oct 2026
Read the research note
Releases studied
45 (2015–2026)
Variance on earnings days
28%
  • Python
  • Jupyter

A quantitative test of a common trading belief: that Netflix moves on earnings and drifts flat in between. The study covers 45 releases from Q3 2015 to Q3 2026 and 44 complete cycles, benchmarked against SPY, QQQ and 13 peers, with an options section built on the historical option data that could be found.

  • Earnings dominate the risk. The 44 reaction days are 1.6% of trading days but carry 28% of daily return variance (95% confidence interval 15% to 39%).
  • But it is not flat in between. Between releases NFLX runs at about 36% annualised volatility, twice SPY’s on the same days.
  • No edge after costs turned up in the options data available.

It is built as a reproducible notebook. Prices come from Yahoo Finance, the earnings calendar from SEC 8-K filings (with before-open or after-close timing taken from each filing’s acceptance time), and implied volatilities are computed with Black-76 from historical option quotes.
